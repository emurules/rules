# Changelog

All notable changes to EMU Rules are documented here.

## v2026.6 Edition

A clarification and discipline pass. It settles points where the text was
silent, ambiguous or inconsistent with itself, fixes the Judge's fixed
phrases, and completes the lists of Warnings, Disqualifying Fouls and PPK
grounds. PPK is renamed **Victory of the Opposite Team (PPK)**, a change of
label only. These changes are of substance:

- At Night, a breach is a Disqualifying Foul or nothing: no Warning is
  given, then or the next Day, so that no Night information is revealed
  (new §12.12). During Relaxed Seating and a technical pause, minor breaches
  draw only a general Notice to the whole table.
- A Night action without the Judge's call is a Disqualifying Foul whether
  or not it is intentional (§12.7.6).
- Talking, deliberately making noise, touching another player, touching the
  mask, involuntary peeking, eating or drinking, and a serious posture
  breach during the Night are Disqualifying Fouls (§12.7.7, §12.7.13–14,
  §12.7.18–19).
- New Disqualifying Fouls for whispering hidden Day information and for
  seeking a non-game advantage (§12.7.15–16), and new PPK grounds (§12.8.3).
- An involuntary emotional response that keeps disrupting play after a
  Notice is a Disqualifying Foul; the Warning step is removed (§12.11).
- Once victory is determined, most sanctions no longer apply (§12.9).
- The Best Move goes only to a first-killed player who is the first to leave
  the game (§8.8).
- One action meeting several penalty triggers draws only the highest
  (§15.1.10).

### Fixed phrases and table procedure

- **§5.1.1, §3.1.2.1** — the Judge opens the Day with "Good morning.",
  mirroring "Night falls" for the Night.
- **§4.2.9, §9.3.1** — on Night 0 the Judge says "Sheriff can show their
  sign." The Sheriff shows the sign without removing the mask. Showing it is
  optional, and no penalty applies if they do not.
- **§4.2.1, §4.2.7, §4.3.2** — the Mafia calls are made plural throughout:
  "Mafia wake up and get acquainted.", "Mafia fall asleep.", "Mafia go
  hunting and raise their pistols."
- **§4.3.9–10** — the shooting closes with "Mafia lower their pistols and fall
  asleep.", the counterpart of the opening call in §4.3.2. The Dark players
  lower their hands at that call.
- **§4.6.2** — the Judge's Best Move call is now "…call up to three numbers
  and nothing else…", since one or two numbers are valid (see *Best Move*).
- **§4.4.1, §9.4.1** — the Don's call is now "Don wakes and does their
  check." Naming the Sheriff in the Don's call had led the real Sheriff to
  open their eyes during the Don's check. New **§9.1.5**: no Night call names
  a role other than the one being woken.
- **§4.4.7, §4.5.8** (new) — the Don and Sheriff calls are made on every
  Night, even after that player has left the game, so that their departure is
  not revealed. A player who wakes on a call that is not theirs commits a
  Night-procedure foul (§12.7.6), intentionally or not.
- **§4.8, §7.6.2, §4.1 note** — a technical pause is held masked, in Relaxed
  Seating: Night behaviour applies, but hands rest visibly on the table
  instead of crossed over the shoulders. New **§4.8.3**: a breach of Night
  behaviour during a technical pause is sanctioned as at Night (§12.12):
  a Disqualifying Foul, or only a general Notice for involuntary peeking,
  touching the mask or posture. v2026.5 made such a breach a Warning.
- **§5.5.4–6** (new) — players address one another by seat number or
  nickname, and the Judge as "Mr/Ms Judge" or "Mr/Ms Host". A speech ends
  with "Pass" or "Thank you". The Judge corrects a breach, and a repeated
  breach may draw a Notice.
- **§5.7.1.4, ¶10** — the Judge's "Stop" / "Thank you" is defined once, as
  the end-of-vote signal, and ¶10 refers to it.

### Day and voting

- **§3.1.2.5, §5.4.3** — where the seat after the previous Day's opener is
  empty, the Day opens with the next player still at the table, in seat
  order.
- **§5.6.3** — one nomination per player per Day, made in the Day discussion
  speech, including the turn of a lost speech (§12.4.4). No nomination in a
  tie speech (§5.8) or a final speech (§5.3).
- **§5.6.5** (new) — the Judge acknowledges a valid nomination with
  "Accepted."
- **§5.7.3** (new) — where no one is nominated, no vote is held and no one
  leaves.
- **§5.8.7** — where the elimination question is barred (three players at
  nine, or every player at the table), it is not put, no one leaves, and the
  Night is announced. v2026.5 said only that the vote "is not conducted".
- **§6.3.3** — the leaving player's final speech after a decisive vote or
  Night kill is an express exception to "no further speeches". The result is
  announced first, and the speech follows.
- **§7.6.4** (new) — the Judge corrects their own procedural error as soon as
  it is noticed, where the game can be restored. Otherwise play continues,
  and the error is recorded for review.

### Masks

- **§2.3.1.3–5, §11.1.7** (new) — the mask is defined as a blindfold: an
  opaque eye cover on an elastic band that covers both eyes completely, with
  no gap. It must fit, and it must stay in place by its band alone, not held
  by hand. "Putting on" and "removing" the mask mean covering and uncovering
  the eyes.

### Best Move

- **§4.6.3, §4.6.6, §13.3.2–3, §13.3.6** — the player may state only seat
  numbers, up to three. Anything else, including asking anyone for help in
  choosing, voids the Best Move for scoring. As information at the table,
  only the first three seat numbers, or fewer, stated before anything else
  remain valid. Fewer than three numbers is still a valid Best Move: it is
  scored against three, and a number not stated counts as wrong.

  Example: the player names 4 and 7 before the 20 seconds run out, and both
  are Dark. That is two of three, so +0.5. Had the player said "4, 7, and
  check 9", the Best Move would be void for scoring. Seats 4 and 7 would
  still stand as information at the table (§4.6.6).

- **§8.8, §11.11.3** — rule change. The first-killed player has a Best Move
  only where they are the first player to leave the game. Any earlier
  departure removes it: a vote-out on any Day, even a single player on Day 1,
  or a removal for a fourth Warning or a Disqualifying Foul. v2026.5 removed
  the Best Move only where two or more players left by the Day 1 vote. The
  player is still first-killed for Compensation Points and the rating
  tie-break.

  Example: one player is voted out on Day 1 and another is killed on Night 1.
  The killed player has no Best Move, but still counts as first-killed for
  Ci. Likewise if Night 1 misfires, a player is voted out on Day 2, and the
  first kill comes on Night 2.
- **§4.6.1, §11.11.2** — wording clarified, with no change in meaning. The
  first-killed player is the first player killed in the game, on whatever
  Night. A misfire kills no one, so it makes no one first-killed. The v2026.5
  wording ("passes to", "does not extinguish") suggested a status that
  existed before the first kill.

### Discipline

- **§12.12** (new) — Sanctions during the Night:
  - ¶1: a breach during the Night draws a Disqualifying Foul (or PPK), or no
    sanction. No Warning and no Notice to a player is given for it, during
    the Night or afterwards: a sanction announced the next Day would reveal
    what happened at Night.
  - ¶2: a posture breach the Judge does not consider serious, and an
    involuntary sound such as a cough or a sneeze, draw no sanction.
  - ¶3–5: during Relaxed Seating and a technical pause, involuntary peeking,
    touching the mask and posture breaches draw only a **general Notice**:
    "Remain in the Night position, please.", said to the whole table without
    naming anyone and recorded against no one. Every other breach in those
    periods, such as talking or touching another player, is sanctioned as at
    Night. The Judge may give the same reminder at any other moment of the
    Night.
  - ¶6: deliberate peeking and non-game methods of obtaining information
    remain Disqualifying Fouls during Relaxed Seating and a technical pause.
  - ¶7: a Disqualifying Foul at Night, in Relaxed Seating or in a technical
    pause is not announced. The Judge removes the player at once and in
    silence, without interrupting the Night and without giving the reason.

  Example: during the Sheriff's check, a Red player lifts a hand from their
  shoulder to adjust their mask. Touching the mask is a Disqualifying Foul:
  the Judge silently removes the player, the Night goes on, and no reason is
  given. During the 30 seconds of Relaxed Seating on Night 0, the same mask
  adjustment draws only the general Notice. If, during the check, another
  player's hand merely slips from their shoulder, that is a Disqualifying
  Foul only if the Judge considers it serious; otherwise it draws no
  sanction, and no Warning is given the next Day.
- **§12.6** — Warnings:
  - ¶8 now covers only refusing, before the Night, to remove a hat, glasses
    or another item that interferes with the mask. v2026.5 ¶8 made any breach
    of Night behaviour a Warning.
  - ¶9 now covers mask handling in the Day only, outside a technical pause.
    v2026.5 also covered Relaxed Seating.
  - ¶11 becomes a real catch-all: other conduct of comparable weight, at the
    Judge's determination. v2026.5 covered only violations "expressly
    defined" elsewhere, which left the Judge no clause for unlisted conduct.
  - New ¶12–14: knocking on the table or unethical conduct toward players or
    Judges; gesturing or campaigning during the vote; refusing to remove the
    mask when the Judge asks.
- **§12.7** — Disqualifying Fouls:
  - ¶4 is limited to information obtained by prohibited means (peeking,
    hearing).
  - ¶6 defines a Night-procedure violation: a Night action (waking, removing
    the mask in order to act, or a Night gesture) other than on the Judge's
    call, including waking on a call addressed to another player. v2026.5
    covered only an *intentional* violation; intent no longer matters.
    Conduct with its own paragraph (mask, noise, peeking, posture) or covered
    by §12.12 is sanctioned there.
  - ¶7: any touch of the mask at Night other than on the Judge's call is a
    Disqualifying Foul, including during the shooting. v2026.5 required the
    eyes to be, or possibly be, exposed.
  - New ¶13–19: talking, or deliberately making noise, at Night; touching
    another player at Night; whispering unique Day information hidden from
    the table; an attempt to gain a non-game advantage to prove a role or
    sway the vote; an involuntary emotional response that keeps disrupting
    play after a Notice, or that disrupts play at Night; involuntary peeking
    at Night; eating or drinking at
    Night, or a posture breach the Judge considers serious. v2026.5 made
    involuntary peeking a Warning, and only by implication.
- **§4.1.8, §12.10.4** — Night behaviour points to §12.12. At Night, in
  Relaxed Seating and in a technical pause, a Notice is given only as a
  general Notice.
- **§12.8** — PPK grounds:
  - Bribery and threats join blackmail and coercion.
  - New grounds: prompts from the audience, and committing §12.7.5, §12.7.9,
    §12.7.15 or §12.7.16 after one's own Night kill is announced or after
    being voted out.
  - The overlapping "intentional disclosure of unique Night information" is
    replaced by a precise ground: a revealed Sheriff proving the role by what
    could be seen during a check. §12.7.4 and §12.8 no longer overlap.
- **§12.3.5–6** — a removed player who then commits a PPK ground receives
  both the Removal and the PPK penalty. ¶5 now points to §12.9.
- **§12.9** — restated. Once victory is determined, the result stands. No
  Disqualifying Foul or PPK is imposed then, except for offensive or abusive
  language, obscene language, the word "suicide", or leaving the table.
  ¶3 (the removal penalty still applies) keeps its number and now refers to
  ¶2 and ¶4. New ¶4: Warnings may still be given, but a fourth Warning only
  for an obvious attempt to disrupt the leaving player's final speech, and
  only after a Notice.

  Example: the Red team wins by the vote on the last Dark player. During that
  player's final speech, another player talks over them. The Judge gives a
  Notice. If the player persists, the Judge may give a Warning, which removes
  them if it is their fourth. A non-game appeal made in the same speech is no
  longer sanctioned.
- **§12.11** — an involuntary emotional response draws a Notice and, if it
  keeps disrupting play, a Disqualifying Foul (§12.7.17). At Night, where no
  Notice can be given, one that disrupts play is a Disqualifying Foul
  straight away. The Warning step and the separate ¶3 are removed.

### Scoring and penalties

- **§11.6, §12.2.4, §12.8, §13.4.4.3, §13.7.2** — "Team Defeat Assignment" is
  renamed **Victory of the Opposite Team (PPK)**. The abbreviation and the
  sanction are unchanged.
- **§13.8.1** — Compensation Points are awarded to a player first-killed as a
  Red player or the Sheriff, in a game the Red team lost or drew. v2026.5
  referred to "specific situations" without listing them.
- **§11.1.8** (new), **§13.8.2** — "rating distance" is defined. It continues
  when the field shrinks while the number of tables stays the same. Any other
  change in the number of participants starts a new distance for that stage.
- **§15.1.10** (new) — where a single action meets several triggers, only the
  highest-valued one applies. The values are not added together: one action,
  one penalty.

  Example: a Red player who votes into another Red player during a Day 1
  agreed split meets §15.4.1 (Big, if Red lost) and §15.4.3 (Small). If Red
  lost, the Big penalty alone applies; if Red won, only the Small one.
- **§15.3.7** — the four cases in which the Sheriff's check information
  counts as not accurate, or the priority as misleading, are now listed.

### Numbering

No sections or paragraphs are renumbered. New items are appended to their
lists: §2.3.1.3–5, §4.4.7, §4.5.8, §4.8.3, §5.5.4–6, §5.6.5, §5.7.3,
§7.6.4, §8.8.2, §9.1.5, §11.1.7–8, §12.3.6, §12.6.12–14, §12.7.13–19,
§12.9.4, §12.10.4, §15.1.10 and §15.3.7.1–4. New §12.12 follows §12.11.
§12.11.3 is removed.

## v2026.5 Edition

The three-Warning speech penalty is restated. Previously §12.4 removed
"further speeches during the current Day phase", which cost nothing to a
player warned after their own speech. The player now loses exactly one
speech: the next discussion speech that falls due, on the current Day or a
later one.

### Changes

- **§12.4** — rule fix, restated in full:
  - ¶1, ¶3: the lost speech is the first discussion speech due after the
    third Warning, today or on a later Day; only that one speech is lost.
  - ¶2 (new): a third Warning given during the player's own speech does not
    cut that speech short; the player finishes it, and the next speech is
    lost.
  - ¶4: in the slot of the lost speech the player may still nominate a
    candidate, and nothing more.
  - ¶5: with three or four players at the table, the speech is shortened to
    thirty seconds instead of lost, and that shortened speech counts as the
    lost one.
  - ¶6: voting, re-vote speech (§5.8) and final speech (§5.3) are unaffected.

  Example: a player speaks in seat order on Day 2, then receives a third
  Warning later in the same discussion. They have no further speech that
  Day, so the lost speech is their Day 3 speech. On Day 4 they speak for the
  full minute again. Had the third Warning come during their own Day 2
  speech, they would have finished that speech as usual, and the Day 3 speech
  would still be lost.
- **§8.4.1** — summary updated to match §12.4.

Apart from §12.4, which is restated in full, no sections or paragraphs are
renumbered.

## v2026.4 Edition

Zero-based Night numbering. Nights are now counted from zero: the Night of
role distribution and the Dark team's agreement is **Night 0**, and the first
shooting Night is **Night 1**. Days keep their numbering: Day 1 still follows
the agreement Night, so Day N now sits between Night N−1 and Night N. The
same Nights simply carry new numbers. Three points are of substance:

- Night 0 is now expressly excluded wherever the Rules count Nights (§8.5,
  §15.2.7, §15.2.13). v2026.3 did not settle this, so a club that counted the
  agreement Night will now reach the draw one Night later.
- §15.2.7 and §15.2.13 no longer require the missed Nights to be consecutive.
  "Consecutive" was a wording error; any two shooting Nights during the game
  without a shot, or without a check, now trigger the Big penalty.
- The Sheriff's endgame exception is defined and extended. §15.2.10 now
  defines when "the entire Dark team has been identified", including where the
  last Dark player can be deduced from the Sheriff's checks and the number of
  players left. §15.2.13 gains the same exception: once that point is reached,
  a Sheriff who stops checking is not penalised for the Nights that follow.

### Mapping from v2026.3

Under v2026.3 the words "first Night" and "Second Night" named different Nights
than they do now. Read any v2026.3 reference through this table:

| v2026.3 wording | Meaning | v2026.4 wording |
|---|---|---|
| "first Night" / "meet-Night" | role distribution + Dark agreement + Sheriff identification | **Night 0** |
| "Second Night" (§11.4) / "second Night onward" | first Night with shooting and checks | **Night 1** / "Night 1 onward" |
| — | second shooting Night | Night 2 |
| "first Day" | the Day after the agreement Night | **Day 1** (same Day, new wording) |

§11.4 now also says that an ordinal word follows the number: "Zero Night"
= Night 0, "First Night" = Night 1, "Second Night" = Night 2. The text itself
uses numerals throughout.

### Changes

- **§2.2.1** — the opening "Night falls" begins Night 0; role distribution is
  part of Night 0.
- **§2.5.4** — rating designation deadline: "before the first Night" → "before
  Night 0 begins" (same moment).
- **§3.1.1** — ¶3–4 restated in Night 0 / Night 1 terms; ¶3 lists everything
  Night 0 holds (role distribution, the Dark agreement, the Sheriff's
  identification), matching §11.4.1. New ¶6: "Night falls" begins every Night
  phase and does not identify a particular Night.
- **§4.2** — retitled "Night 0 — …". ¶1 corrected: the Judge does not *open*
  a Night with "Mafia wakes up and gets acquainted"; that call is made within
  Night 0, once role distribution is complete. ¶5: the shooting order applies
  "from Night 1 onward" (was "on subsequent Nights").
- **§4.3** — retitled "Night 1 and Subsequent Nights — …". ¶1 no longer says
  "like the first", which implied the two Nights opened differently.
- **§8.5** — the three-Night draw now counts shooting Nights only; Night 0 is
  expressly excluded. v2026.3 did not say whether the agreement Night counted.
- **§11.4** — retitled from "'Second Night' Definition" to "Night and Day
  Numbering"; defines Night 0, Night 1, Day numbering, and ordinal words.
- **§15.2.7 / §15.2.13** — rule fix. "Two or more consecutive Nights" without
  shooting, or without a check, becomes "two or more shooting Nights during the
  game", consecutive or not. The word "consecutive" was a wording error present
  since the first edition. Only shooting Nights count (Night 1 onward); Night 0
  has no shooting and no checks, so it can never make up one of the two Nights.
- **§15.2.10** — "the entire Dark team has been identified" is now defined: the
  Sheriff's previous checks, alone or with the number of players left at the
  table, already establish every remaining player's team, so no further check
  could tell the Sheriff anything new. This covers, for instance, a Sheriff who
  has checked all three Dark players, and a table of four (the Sheriff, two
  checked Reds, one unchecked player) where the unchecked player can only be
  Dark. A closing sentence separates this test, which concerns the Sheriff's
  own knowledge, from "certainly identified" at the table under §11.10.
- **§15.2.13** — new endgame exception, using the §15.2.10 definition: a Night
  that falls after the entire Dark team has been identified is not counted.
  With nothing left to find, the Sheriff has no reason to keep checking. Missed
  checks before that point still count.
- **§9.3, §15.2.1–3, §15.2.11–12, §15.3.3, §16.2.2, §16.3.2** — Night references
  restated as Night 0 / Night 1.
- **§3.1.2.4, §5.4.2, §5.7.2.1, §8.8.1, §15.2.3, §15.2.12, §15.3.3, §15.4.1** —
  "first Day" → "Day 1" (wording only).

No sections or paragraphs are renumbered.

## v2026.3 Edition

A clarity-and-consistency pass. Every point below was raised by cross-checking
the ruleset against real judging practice; the
document's own preamble requires such fixes to be made in the Rules themselves,
under a new version.

### New Part

- **PART XVI — Gesture Reference** added. PARTS IV and IX repeatedly referred to
  "the official gesture established by these Rules" without any clause, figure,
  or table describing those gestures. PART XVI now describes each one in words:
  the Sheriff sign, the Don sign, the two Don-check results, the two
  Sheriff-check results, and the shot-miming motion. The references in §4.2,
  §4.3, §4.4, §4.5, §9.2, and §9.3–§9.6 now point to it.

### Gameplay clarifications

- **§2.5** — a game now counts for rating by default; a non-rating or tournament
  game must be designated as such before the first Night.
- **§9.2** — during role distribution the Judge now gives the role gesture with
  each card: a raised thumb for a Red card, a lowered thumb for a Dark card, and
  the Sheriff or Don sign for the special roles. The previous clause forbidding
  any gesture beyond showing the card is removed.
- **§4.5 / §9.5** — the Sheriff check result is now a hand gesture only (thumb up
  for Red, thumb down for Dark). The mandatory head-movement component is
  removed. The Don check result keeps its head movement.
- **§4.3.5 / §9.6** — the shot-miming motion is described plainly: two fingers of
  a raised hand held high, timed to the called seat number, direction
  immaterial. A misfire still results only from firing on a different number,
  firing on more than one number, or not firing.
- **§4.6** — the Best Move right is stated to attach to whichever player is first
  killed by a Night shot, whatever Night that falls on; an intervening misfire
  does not extinguish it.
- **§4.7 / §6.3** — win-condition checks use the moment a vote or Night kill is
  *determined*, not the player's physical departure or final speech.
- **§5.8 (Split Vote)** — reworded. Each re-vote is among the players currently
  tied; the narrow-and-re-vote step repeats only while the tied set keeps
  shrinking (it always terminates: the set strictly shrinks and cannot fall
  below two); the eliminate-all question and its effect are about the players
  *currently tied*, not the original nomination list; a non-vote in a re-vote
  falls on the last-nominated player still tied. The "three at nine" / "all
  players" carve-outs are now a pointer to §8.6.
- **§8.1** — where a removal cancels the vote, the rest of the Day still runs and
  only the vote is skipped. Where the removed player was themselves the leading
  candidate, the Judge records the vote as determined at the moment of removal.
- **§8.5** — unchanged in substance; a note confirms that a player leaving for
  any reason, including a removal, resets the three-Night draw count.

### Consistency

- **§8.4** reduced to a pointer to §12.4, which holds the full statement of the
  three-Warning speech rules (previously stated in both places in slightly
  different words).
- **§13.7.4** now cites §12.7.5 and §12.7.10 instead of the phrase "profanity or
  insults", which matched no foul definition.
- **§12.10 ↔ §13.11** cross-reference each other so the Notice and the
  Disciplinary penalty are not conflated.
- **§8.6** notes that its restrictions are restated at §5.8.7.

### Severity reclassifications

- **Concealing speech information in another language** moved from a
  Disqualifying Foul to a Warning (now §12.6.10).
- **Crying at the gaming table** is no longer an automatic Disqualifying Foul.
  New **§12.11** makes an involuntary emotional response a Judge-discretion
  matter: Notice, then Warning if it disrupts play, and removal only if play
  cannot continue.
- **Night mask contact** — §12.6.9 (a Warning) is narrowed to Relaxed Seating
  and the Day. A new Disqualifying Foul (§12.7.7) covers mask contact during the
  Night where the eyes are or could be exposed.

### §12.7 renumbering

Removing two items and inserting one renumbers the Disqualifying Fouls list:

| Old | Content | New |
|---|---|---|
| §12.7.1–6 | peeking; non-game methods; unauthorised gestures; revealing role info; offensive/abusive language; intentional Night-procedure violation | §12.7.1–6 (unchanged) |
| — | **new** — Night mask contact with eyes exposed/exposable | §12.7.7 |
| §12.7.7 | any action compromising integrity | §12.7.8 |
| §12.7.8 | religious/ethical/non-game appeals | §12.7.9 |
| §12.7.9 | concealing speech info in a foreign language | removed → §12.6.10 (now a Warning) |
| §12.7.10 | crying at the table | removed → §12.11 (Judge discretion) |
| §12.7.11 | obscene language | §12.7.10 |
| §12.7.12 | use of "suicide" | §12.7.11 |
| §12.7.13 | leaving the table without permission | §12.7.12 |

### Divergences from FIIM

`DIVERGENCES.md` gains rows for the foreign-language downgrade, the crying
reclassification, the Night mask bright line, the Sheriff-check gesture (EMU
drops the head movement FIIM keeps), and the role-distribution gesture (EMU has
the Judge show the role gesture with each card). The §5.8 and §8.5 changes are
clarifications of FIIM's own wording, not divergences.

## v2026.2 Edition
- **1.2 Scope of Application** rewritten. The section previously held eight
  venue, equipment, and player-conduct clauses under a heading that never
  described their content. All eight were already restated in Part II, so
  the list is removed and 1.2 now states the actual scope of the Rules:
  what format they govern, when they apply, and how they interact with an

  one distinct point ("at all times") is folded into 2.1.3.
- No section renumbering. 2.3 now contains 2.3.1 (Masks) and 2.3.2 (Music).
